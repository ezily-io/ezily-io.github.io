Conversion Best Practices

Customers tap through for a reason. They saw a friend open a group buy, saw a creator recommend something, saw a notice that the window is closing, and they arrive holding one specific thought.

Then they land on a page built for everyone, and the thought is gone.

Four things that keep happening

They arrive with a reason and get a generic page

What they wanted to see was who else had joined. What you gave them was your brand story. That asks them to talk themselves into it a second time, and most people won't.

Your checkout page asks them to register

They're already in LINE. You already know who they are. Then you ask for a name, a phone number, an address. Every extra field is one more reason to leave.

Your LINE friends have no path to a purchase

The message goes out, someone's interested, and then they leave LINE for your website and log in again. Every redirect costs you people.

Discount becomes the only tool you have

Sales stall, so you discount. Discounting costs margin, and customers learn to wait for the next one. Conversion turns into a race you can only run downhill.

All four share a shape: the intent was there, and friction along the way wore it down.

What we've found works

Match the destination to the reason they came

Take one product. Someone arriving from a friend's group buy link, someone from a creator's post, and someone from a closing-soon notice all want to see something different first. Who else joined. What the creator said. How much time is left.

Send all three to the same page and each of them has to re-sell themselves. Different entry point, different landing.

Don't introduce yourself again at checkout

They're already in LINE and you already know who they are. Asking them to fill everything in from scratch throws away the relationship you just built.

One-click checkout isn't really about speed. It's about the reason they came not breaking at the last step.

Group buy runs on the organizer

The person who opens a group buy is doing marketing you cannot buy. What they need is simple: one link, visibility into who joined, and no collecting money themselves.

If organizing feels like work, there won't be a second one. Design the group buy around the organizer first and the product second.

If it's timed, mean it

Flash sales work because they convert "let me think about it" into "decide now."

If your timed event is always running, customers learn to ignore it. The value comes from it actually ending. Close it when it closes, so the next one still works.

Let tags decide who sees what

Not everyone needs to see a discount. A frequent repeat buyer and a first-time visitor should be looking at different things.

A VIP event uses tags to decide who gets in, which turns price into a form of standing rather than an across-the-board markdown. This is where the tags from the engagement layer actually earn their keep.

Treat the sale as the beginning

A shipping notification is a message customers open. That's your moment to ask how it's working out, suggest an add-on, or ask them to share it.

Closing the sale isn't the end of the flow. It's the point where you know the most about someone, so don't waste it.

Common mistakes

Routing all traffic to the homepage The homepage is for people who don't know what they want. Someone arriving from a group buy link knows. Don't make them look again.

Still requiring registration at checkout Registration is your requirement, not theirs. Let them buy, and talk about membership later.

Using discount as the only lever A discount rescues one campaign. It doesn't fix conversion. Timing, standing, and social pressure are all levers too, and none of them costs margin.

A timed event that never ends The second time someone sees "last day," they stop believing the third.

Opening a group buy and walking away The momentum is in the days right after it opens. With nobody posting progress or saying it's nearly full, it just goes cold.

See it running

The conversion layer in Ezily Touchpoints covers group buy, flash sale, VIP event, and one-click checkout. Every entry point can have its own destination, with layout, messaging, and product mix matched to it. LINE is the first surface, and the same approach isn't bound to LINE.

Paired with the tags from the engagement layer, you decide who sees which price, and when.
