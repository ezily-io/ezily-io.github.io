Four things that keep happening

Intent is usually there. Friction along the way wears it down.

01

They arrive with a reason and get a generic page

What they wanted was "who else joined." What you showed them was your homepage brand story. That asks them to convince themselves again, and most won't.

02

Checkout asks them to register

They're already in LINE. You already know who they are. Then you ask for name, phone, address. Every extra field is one more reason to leave.

03

LINE friends have no path to purchase

The message goes out, someone's interested, then they leave LINE for your website and log in again. Every redirect costs you people.

04

Discount becomes the only tool

Sales stall, so you discount. Discounting costs margin, and customers learn to wait. Conversion turns into a race you can only run downhill.

The problem isn't lack of intent.The road is too long.

Remove unnecessary redirects, re-entry, and re-understanding. Let the original reason carry all the way to the sale.

What we've found works

Keep intent from wearing out.

From entry to checkout to community to timing to identity to post-sale, each step must carry forward the reason from the last.

  1. 01

    Match the destination to the reason they came

    Group buy, creator post, closing-soon alert. Three entry points, three different things customers want to see first. Different entry, different landing.

    One entry, one destination
  2. 02

    Don't re-introduce yourself at checkout

    They're already in LINE. Don't throw away the relationship you just built. One-click checkout isn't about speed; it's about the reason not breaking at the last step.

    Carry the existing identity
  3. 03

    Group buy runs on the organizer

    Give them one link, visibility into who joined, and no money collecting. Design the group buy around the organizer first, the product second.

    Make the second time easier
  4. 04

    If it's timed, mean it

    A flash sale converts "let me think" into "decide now." But it has to actually end. Close it when it closes, so the next one still works.

    Time must be credible
  5. 05

    Let tags decide who sees what

    A repeat buyer and a first-time visitor shouldn't see the same offer. VIP events turn price into standing, not an across-the-board cut.

    Put engagement tags to work
  6. 06

    Treat the sale as the beginning

    A shipping notification is a message customers always open. That's the moment to ask how it's going, suggest an add-on, or invite a share.

    Post-sale, data is richest

One unbroken path

Let the reason reach the next purchase

Good conversion doesn't end at checkout. It turns every sale into the start of the next relationship.

01 / Entry

Preserve the reason

Know whether they arrived from a group buy, a recommendation, or a closing-soon alert.

02 / Landing

Answer the immediate question

Show the proof and timing they care about most, before anything else.

03 / Checkout

No re-entry, no redirect

Use the LINE identity they already have. Keep friction outside the flow.

04 / Post-sale

Catch the next interaction

At shipping and first use, keep building the relationship and the tags.

Friction hides in familiar flows

Common mistakes

Expand each item for the fix.

Routing all traffic to the homepage

The homepage is for people who don't know what they want. Someone arriving from a group buy link knows. Don't make them look again.

Still requiring registration at checkout

Registration is your need, not theirs. Let them buy first, and talk about membership later.

Using discount as the only lever

A discount rescues one campaign. It doesn't fix conversion. Timing, standing, and social proof are all levers, and none costs margin.

A timed event that never ends

The second time someone sees "last day," they stop believing the third.

Opening a group buy and walking away

The momentum is in the days right after it opens. With nobody posting progress or saying it's nearly full, it just goes cold.
