Four places campaign traffic stops converting

The buying intent your brand worked to build disappears the moment someone lands on a generic product page.

The group buy link lands on your homepage

They want to see who joined and what is left. They get your brand story.

Creator traffic loses the thread

Her reasons stay in the post. The click lands on a standard product page.

Flash sales share the regular product page

The countdown lives in a banner. No cutoff, no stock count in sight.

The checkout path is too long

Leave LINE, log in, find it, type an address. By then the urge is gone.

The intent is there.Everyone just lands on the same generic site.

Group buy visitors need progress. Creator traffic needs context. Flash sale visitors need a deadline.

Every campaign, its own checkout

Group buy, flash sale, VIP event, one-click checkout. Each lays out its own page and product mix.

  1. Group buy pages lead with progress, not product

    Who joined, spots left, and the deadline sit at the top.

  2. Flash sale pages make the deadline real

    Countdown and stock left come before the description.

    Time is layout, not a banner

    Repeat buyers see their own price, not a sitewide markdown.

  4. One-click checkout turns six steps into one

    Checkout happens inside the store

  5. Creator traffic lands on the creator's store

    Her three picks, her reasons, her bundle, all on the landing page.

    Context carries from post to storefront

  6. After the sale, the data stays with you

    Where they came in and what they bought, kept on your side.

    Every storefront teaches you more about your customers

Common mistakes

Expand each item for a more direct fix.

Every campaign links to the same page

Group buy visitors want progress. Creator traffic wants the recommendation. Flash sale visitors want a countdown. Your homepage answers none of those questions.

The flash sale countdown lives only in a banner

The countdown sits in a banner and the product page stays the same. Customers feel no urgency, and they will not believe next time is really the last day.

Creator traffic loses context after the click

She spent an entire post explaining why she picked these three. They click through and see your standard product page. The recommendation resets to zero.

Checkout requires leaving LINE and logging in again

Every redirect asks the customer the same question. Do you still want to buy? Each extra step gives them another reason to leave.

The group buy launches and nobody follows up

The momentum peaks in the first few days. Without progress updates or nearly-full alerts, the group cools on its own. A purpose-built group buy page updates itself.
