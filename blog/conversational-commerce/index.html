LINE Broadcasts Are Getting Pricey. In 2026, Close the Sale Inside the Conversation

Broadcasting to 20,000 LINE friends costs an extra ~NT$10,000 a month yet still misses real buyers. In 2026, stop broadcasting — capture intent and close the sale inside each LINE conversation with registration-free checkout.

It's 11 p.m. and you're still answering DMs one by one. "Do you still have this in M?" "I want two — can you combine shipping?" "Paid, last four digits 3312." The messages pour in like a waterfall while you screenshot, jot things into a notepad, and reconcile payments until your eyes blur. The next morning you open your back office and find three orders you missed — one customer already bought elsewhere. This isn't a one-off. It's the reality that social sellers push to the top of Threads every few days: DM-order hell.

So a lot of people think: "Fine, I'll just use broadcast instead" — push every new product and promo to all your friends at once, and skip replying one by one. But here's the mistake Taiwanese e-commerce brands most easily make in 2026: a broadcast is a megaphone with a worse and worse return on investment. You shout at twenty thousand people, and the one who actually wants to buy gets drowned out in the noise. This article isn't about how to send LINE broadcasts more often or more cheaply. It's about something more important: stop broadcasting to everyone, and start turning purchase intent into an order right inside each conversation.

The bottom line first: LINE broadcasts are getting both pricier and less effective

Under the 2026 pricing, broadcasting to 20,000 people costs ~NT$10,000 more a month

Start with a number that will sober you up. According to the estimate in actgsys's "LINE Bot Costs 2026," LINE Official Accounts move to new pricing on November 1, 2026: light tier NT$0/month with only 200 free messages; medium tier NT$1,000 for 3,000 messages; heavy tier NT$1,400 for 6,000 messages, with the first 50,000 overage messages at NT$0.2 each. Do the math on the same 20,000 friends: blast them indiscriminately and it costs about NT$15,450 a month; send to a targeted segment of only the relevant people and it's about NT$4,800 — a gap of NT$10,650 a month. (Source: actgsys "LINE Bot Costs 2026," 2026.) One more note: existing accounts must adjust before October 27, 2026, or the new pricing applies automatically.

That gap is worth pausing on. For the same campaign, the extra ten thousand dollars doesn't buy you more orders — it just stuffs your message into more phones belonging to people who were never going to buy. Instead of paying to shout at everyone, put that budget toward capturing the people who actually have intent. That's exactly where Ezily comes in: use Touchpoints to catch intent inside the conversation, then use registration-free Checkout (your LINE identity *is* your membership) to turn that conversation straight into an order — and spend what you save on things that actually convert.

Worse than the cost: shouting at everyone misses the one who wants to buy

A broadcast is one-way by nature: one message, one push, aimed at an entire list. The problem is that as your friend count piles up, broadcast opens and clicks usually get weaker than before — the list fills with people who went inactive long ago, aren't a fit, or only added you for a one-time coupon, so your reach is diluted. (This is a widely shared qualitative observation; there is no dated public statistic in Taiwan that can tell you exactly how many percentage points it has fallen, so we'll talk trend here and not hand you a fake number.)

But the real pain was never "not sending enough." It's that the moment someone thinks "I want this," a broadcast can't catch them at all. A broadcast shouts into the air, while purchase intent is fleeting. What you need isn't a bigger loudspeaker — it's a pair of hands that can catch intent inside the conversation.

What collapses even earlier than broadcast: DM-order taking

Reconciling until your eyes blur, handwritten orders, missed sales

Rewind to the opening scene. Before broadcasts got pricey and ineffective, most social sellers were stuck in an even more primitive hell: taking orders purely through DMs. A customer says "I want this one," and you manually note the item, size, quantity, and address, screenshot the payment proof, verify the last digits by hand, and copy it all into an ever-growing Google spreadsheet. Add more SKUs and a dozen customers arriving at once, and the traffic jam begins: reply too slowly and the customer leaves; reconcile wrong and you ship the wrong thing; miss one line in a handwritten order and a sale vanishes into thin air.

This "brute-force" way of taking orders is fine when you have five orders a day. But the moment business picks up and scale arrives, it crashes right on schedule. The time you spend reconciling, re-entering, and apologizing far outweighs the time you spend actually selling.

DMs and broadcasts are two sides of the same hole

Have you noticed that DM order-taking and broadcasting look like two extremes but are really two sides of the same hole? DMs are too manual — everything is handled by hand and can't survive scale. Broadcasts are too scattershot — shouting at everyone, catching no one's individual intent. What they share: neither turns intent into a real order at the moment it appears.

One misses by being too slow, the other by being too loud. To close that hole, the answer isn't "reply to DMs faster" or "broadcast more cheaply" — it's a completely different play.

The 2026 play: turn intent into an order inside every LINE conversation

"Conversational checkout" means moving the last step of a purchase out of a website page and into the chat window: the customer asks, picks, orders, and pays all in one LINE conversation — no redirect, no re-registration. That's the core of conversational commerce — the sale happens in the moment of the conversation, not on some landing page that still has to re-route traffic.

The market has already shifted toward "closing in the chat"

The good news: consumers were ready long ago. Per conversational-commerce market observations for 2026, over 67% of Taiwanese consumers now prefer to complete purchases in a chat interface rather than by browsing a traditional website; buying behavior is shifting wholesale from "search, then order" to "close the sale in the conversation itself." (Source: actgsys / udn, Conversational Commerce 2026.)

This is a pivotal mindset shift: the chat box is no longer just a support window — it's the point of sale. As consumers grow used to asking, picking, and buying inside chat, brands that still pour traffic back to a website, demand re-registration, and ask people to fill out forms are putting a roadblock at the very last step of the sale.

Intent has a shelf life: hesitate and you lose the order

And that "in-the-moment" window is shorter than you think. Omnichat's "Conversational Commerce 2026" research notes that about 66% of consumers globally are ready to complete a purchase directly in chat — but a reply delayed by five minutes can cut purchase-completion rates by up to about 56%. (Source: Omnichat, "Conversational Commerce 2026: Turning Chats into Revenue," 2026.) The same research projects the conversational-commerce market will reach US$12.64 billion in 2026, with retail making up roughly 40%.

Burn this into your memory: intent has a shelf life. Five minutes late and you lose more than half your chance to close. Broadcasts are delayed — you schedule, you wait for the campaign to launch, you wait for the customer to find time to look. What actually wins is a play that catches that moment of impulse in real time, inside the conversation. This echoes Ezily's core belief: the sale happens in the moment, not on the page.

How to upgrade from "broadcast / DMs" to "conversational checkout": the Ezily Conversational Close Flywheel

To upgrade messy DMs and inefficient broadcasts into systematic conversational checkout, Ezily uses a method we call the Conversational Close Flywheel: catch intent → check out on the spot → re-engage by segment → accumulate first-party data — four steps that interlock and spin faster over time, because this campaign's conversation data feeds the next campaign's segmentation. Here's the step-by-step.

Step 1 — Use Touchpoints to catch intent (instead of passively waiting for DMs)

Stop passively waiting for customers to DM you. Turn every traffic entry point into a LINE conversation that automatically catches intent. That's what Ezily's Touchpoints do: a click from your ad, a "beep" from the NFC tag at your store counter, a link a customer shared — all converge into the same LINE conversation. Inside that conversation, interactive quizzes and AI product discovery guide the customer as they browse — they answer preferences and make choices while you accumulate their purchase intent and segmentation tags. Passive DM-waiting becomes active intent-catching. (To gauge whether your conversation experience is smart enough and where it's stuck, start with a self-diagnosis using [the Visibility Layers](/blog/line-chatbot-visibility-layers-2026).)

Step 2 — Registration-free Checkout: turn the conversation into an order on the spot

This is the "last mile" that broadcast tutorials never reach — and it matters most. When a customer says "this one" in the conversation, Ezily's Checkout lets them stay in LINE — no jumping to a website, no re-registering an account. Your LINE identity is your membership, and checkout completes right in the conversation. Remember "a five-minute delay cuts conversion by 56%"? The point of registration-free checkout is to shrink the distance between "the second they want it" and "payment done" to the minimum, giving intent no chance to cool. This ability to check out directly inside LINE is one of the shifts brought by [the LINE Mini App's new era for e-commerce](/blog/line-mini-app-ecommerce-taiwan).

Step 3 — Replace broadcasts with segments: cheaper and more precise

Back to the math from the top of this article: broadcasting to 20,000 people costs about NT$15,450 a month; a targeted segment costs about NT$4,800. That ten-thousand-dollar gap is the dead tax you pay to "shout at everyone." Replace indiscriminate broadcasts with first-party-data-driven segmented re-engagement — send only to people whose tags and intent line up, for lower cost and higher conversion. For how to structure your tags and design automated pushes, see our [guide to segmentation tag architecture](/blog/line-crm-tags).

Step 4 — Let conversations keep accumulating first-party data

Finally, and most easily overlooked: make every conversation and order a retainable asset. First-party data here means data you collect directly from customer interactions, without passing through a third party. In Ezily, every interaction and transaction is bound to the customer's LINE UID, steadily building an ever-clearer customer profile. Who to target next campaign, what to push, what messaging to use — no longer a guess, but a read of the data. That way, one-off traffic is converted into a relationship you can work again and again. To see how brands actually retain customers rather than just build a pretty website, these [Taiwan Shopify cases](/blog/shopify-taiwan-cases-line-retention) are worth a read.

FAQ

Q1: What's the maximum number of people a LINE broadcast can reach at once? With a personal LINE account's "share message" feature, you can send to at most 15 people at once, with a cap of 20 shares — and it's better suited to ad hoc notices. For true e-commerce-scale reach, use a LINE Official Account's broadcast or segmented push — there's no hard per-person cap like a personal account; it's billed by message count. But as this article argues, the point isn't how many people you can reach — it's whether you can catch the one who wants to buy.

Q2: What's the difference between a LINE broadcast and a segmented push? Why is segmenting cheaper? A broadcast sends "indiscriminately to your entire friend list"; segmenting "sends only to relevant people by tag." Because Official Accounts bill by "messages sent," broadcasting means paying for a crowd that won't buy; segmenting targets a high-intent list, cutting total sends sharply and costing less. For 20,000 friends, the two can differ by about NT$10,000 a month.

Q3: How is the 2026 LINE Official Account broadcast cost calculated? Per actgsys's "LINE Bot Costs 2026," the November 1, 2026 pricing is: light tier NT$0 (incl. 200 free messages), medium tier NT$1,000 for 3,000 messages, heavy tier NT$1,400 for 6,000 messages, with the first 50,000 overage messages at NT$0.2 each. Blasting the same 20,000 friends costs about NT$15,450/month; segmenting is about NT$4,800/month. Existing accounts must adjust before October 27, 2026.

Q4: Can customers check out directly in LINE without registering? Yes. With Ezily's Checkout, for example, the customer doesn't need to leave LINE, connect to a website, or re-register as a member — the system treats their LINE identity as their membership, letting them complete checkout right in the conversation. This dramatically shortens the distance from "want it" to "paid," so intent isn't lost in redirects and sign-ups.

Q5: How does a social seller's DM order-taking become systematic conversational ordering? The key is swapping "human order-logging" for "system order-taking." Touchpoints funnel traffic into one LINE conversation; interactive quizzes and AI product discovery guide product selection; once confirmed, checkout happens registration-free right in the conversation, the order is created automatically, and the data is bound to the LINE UID automatically. No more copying items by hand or reconciling manually — and the missed-order and traffic-jam problems resolve with it.

Q6: My broadcast open rates keep dropping — what should I do? Rather than trying to "rescue" the broadcast, flip the direction: falling open rates usually mean your list is full of people who aren't a fit. The right fix is first-party-data segmentation — send only to relevant people; and further, shift your center of gravity from "one-way push" to "two-way conversational checkout," catching intent the moment a customer engages. Catching one person who genuinely wants to buy beats being read-and-ignored by a hundred.

Conclusion: stop broadcasting, start catching every purchase moment

In 2026, Taiwanese e-commerce isn't won by "broadcasting more cheaply or more widely" — it's won by whether you can turn intent into an order, on the spot, inside a single conversation. A broadcast is a megaphone with a worse and worse ROI: pricier, louder, yet less and less able to catch the one who truly wants to buy. DM order-taking, meanwhile, jams right on schedule once you scale. The shared breach in both is the failure to close the sale "at the moment intent appears."

The real upgrade is to stop broadcasting to everyone and start catching every purchase moment: use Touchpoints to catch intent, registration-free Checkout to close on the spot, segmentation to replace broadcasts, and let first-party data keep accumulating in every conversation. The sale happens in the moment, not on the page.
