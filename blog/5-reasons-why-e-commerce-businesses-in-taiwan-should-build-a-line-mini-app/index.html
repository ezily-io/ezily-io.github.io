Five reasons Taiwan e-commerce brands should build a LINE Mini App

No download, no signup, and service messages that don't depend on friend status. All five in one post.

EzilyLINE · Mini App

On this page

    Most brand apps have the same biography. Ship it, buy ads for installs, run a campaign to push downloads, then watch it sit quietly in a folder on the fourth home screen.

    The app is rarely the problem. The problem is asking someone who is still deciding to install something first. They have to find it, wait for the download, grant permissions, create an account, and enter a verification code before they ever reach your product page. People drop at every one of those steps, and they drop before they have seen anything worth buying.

    A LINE Mini App removes that stretch of road. It is not an app you install; it is a service running inside LINE. For what it is and how it differs from LIFF, see our [LINE Mini App e-commerce guide](/line-mini-app). This post covers the five reasons.

    1. No download, no signup

    A customer taps a link and your service opens inside LINE. No app store, no wait, no registration form.

    Login disappears too. A Mini App carries the customer's LINE identity in, so nobody sees the "enter your phone number and we'll text you a code" screen. For someone who just arrived from an ad and has not decided anything yet, that screen is reason enough to leave.

    Every step you remove takes a moment of hesitation with it. Nothing to download, nothing to register, no password to remember, and the only actions left are browsing and buying.

    2. Your message sits where their friends' messages sit

    Email needs an address you had to earn first, and then it needs to survive the promotions tab. SMS is plain text, one sentence long, and cannot carry browsing or selection.

    LINE is different because a brand's message lands in the same list as messages from family and friends, in an app people in Taiwan already open all day.

    The bigger difference is that the message can carry the whole flow. Email can only offer a link, and the link throws people out into a browser where they sign in again. Tapping a LINE message opens the Mini App with identity intact, cart intact, and the last thing they looked at still there.

    Wherever the message lands, the sale can happen right there.

    3. It connects to your LINE Official Account

    If your brand already talks to customers through a LINE Official Account, the Mini App supplies the button that lets them do something about it.

    Most Official Accounts today can only broadcast: send a message, attach a link, hope for a tap out to the website. That hop is where people are lost.

    Once a Mini App is connected, rich menus and rich messages open it directly, and the customer finishes the task without leaving LINE:

    • Open a membership card, check points, claim a coupon
    • Fill in a survey or a preference quiz
    • Book a slot
    • Check an order or shipping status
    • Start a return, register a warranty

    This is an integration LINE supports directly. You link the Official Account and the Mini App under one identity in the developer console rather than bolting on a workaround.

    For the customer, the difference is between reading a message and finishing an errand.

    4. Service messages don't depend on friend status

    This is the biggest structural difference between a Mini App and ordinary Official Account broadcasting, and the least known.

    A normal broadcast only reaches people who added you as a friend. Blocked you, never added you, added you and left: those people are gone.

    A Mini App's service messages work differently. They follow the interaction rather than the friendship, so order confirmations, shipping notices, booking reminders, and e-receipts still arrive for someone who is not a friend of the account.

    Customers read these as a different category from promotions. A promotion is you wanting to talk. A service message is something they were already waiting for. Someone expecting a package opens the shipping notice and does not feel interrupted.

    For the brand, that is a return path that does not run on ad budget. Every shipping notice is another chance to bring someone back into LINE and back into your Mini App.

    5. Scattered behavior resolves to one person

    Customers in Taiwan do not take one route. They see it on Instagram, order on Shopee, collect in store, and ask a question on LINE a few days later. To the brand that is four unrelated records. To the customer it was one purchase.

    Every Mini App session maps to the same LINE user identity. So the online order, the in-store QR scan, and the link a friend forwarded are all recognizably the same person.

    OMO runs on that identity layer. Points are one balance across store and web, a return can start from anywhere, and the next recommendation follows what someone looked at and bought rather than which door they came through.

    Online and offline see one person, not two records.

    Where to start

    If your Official Account already has a sizable friend list but every broadcast ends at an open rate, that gap is the one a Mini App closes: give the message something to do, turn what people did into tags, and let the tags decide what the next message says.

    You do not have to build all of it at once. Pick one thing customers are already asking for, whether that is order lookup, a membership card, or warranty registration, put it in the Mini App, and see how many people use it.
